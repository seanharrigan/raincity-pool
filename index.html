How it works
R32 Seating: The 495-Combo Problem

The setup

The 2026 World Cup has 12 groups. The top 2 from each group advance automatically (24 teams). The 8 best 3rd-place teams across all groups also qualify — filling the Round of 32.

But here's the twist: which 8 groups produce those third-place qualifiers determines their bracket position. FIFA pre-defined all possible combinations using Annex C of the tournament regulations.

The maths

Choosing 8 groups from 12 gives C(12,8) = 495 possible combinations. For each of those 495 outcomes, Annex C maps each 3rd-place team to a specific R32 slot — ensuring no group plays itself and the bracket stays balanced.

Example — suppose groups A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H qualify

3rd-place team from Assigned R32 slot Plays against
Group ASlot 51st of Group E
Group BSlot 31st of Group C
Group CSlot 82nd of Group H
Group DSlot 21st of Group B
Group ESlot 62nd of Group F
Group FSlot 11st of Group A
Group GSlot 72nd of Group G
Group HSlot 41st of Group D

The actual slots shift depending on which combination of groups qualifies — this is just one of 495 possible layouts.

What this means for you

Until all group stages finish, we won't know which 8 groups contributed 3rd-place qualifiers — so the bracket can't be fully filled. Once groups end, the Annex C table is looked up and all 32 R32 matchups lock in simultaneously.

The bracket updates live after every group stage match as standings shift — it will keep projecting the most likely R32 seeding until group play is complete and the real assignments lock in.

⚠️

We believe this bracket and seating logic is correct, but it's complex stuff — if something looks off, it may need an update. Don't hesitate to flag it.
